CPU-Level Deep Neural Network Optimization

A thesis project for people who look at expensive GPUs and ask, "but what if no?"

CSE498R Thesis Systems Deep Learning

This project investigates CPU-only deep learning performance across vision, NLP, and tokenization workloads. It focuses on multi-core threading, MKL-accelerated tensor operations, and cache-aware memory layout, because sometimes the hardware budget is a research question.

Workloads
U-Net training, BERT-base CPU training, and tokenizer optimization.
U-Net Result
Training time reduced by 35%, from 1924.4s to 1254.0s, with 0.8686 validation Dice.
BERT Result
CPU training time reduced by 83%, from 1033.27s to 171.74s.

Optimization Method

The framework applies multi-core parallelization and memory-conscious computation patterns to reduce training time without changing the fundamental model objectives. It treats CPU execution not as a sad fallback, but as a constrained system worth measuring properly.

Vision Experiment

U-Net training was scaled across 32 CPU cores on a 31M-parameter model. The resulting speedup preserved validation quality while cutting wall-clock training time, which is the kind of result that makes a profiler feel briefly loved.

NLP Experiment

BERT-base CPU training received large gains through multi-core parallelization, with additional improvements from tokenizer-level optimization. The project therefore treats preprocessing as part of the system, not a footnote where performance goes to hide.
